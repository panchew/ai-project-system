## The known-collisions decision, and why option (a)

The spec offered two ways to handle a test that fails on arrival because `SN-23` and `SN-1`
are already double-claimed: **(a)** land it xfailed with a reason, or **(b)** land it
hard-failing on an unmerged branch. **Option (a) was chosen**, as the spec recommended, and
implemented as `@pytest.mark.xfail(strict=True)`.

`strict=True` is the load-bearing detail. Once M36 (E36.1's citation fix, E36.2's renumber)
clears the collisions, the check will **XPASS — and a strict xfail turns an unexpected pass
into a failure.** That forces M36 to remove the marker rather than leaving a
permanently-tolerated failure in the suite. It converts M36's citation cleanup from a
judgment call into a mechanical pass/fail signal, which is what the hotfix was for.

Option (b) was rejected because it puts the guard *outside* the repository until M36 lands,
which defeats the entire point of classifying this as a hotfix.

## What was verified, beyond the DoD

`test_both_known_collisions_are_reported` asserts the detector finds **exactly** `SN-23` and
`SN-1` — no more, no fewer. It passes, which independently confirms SN-28's hand audit
(28 IDs across 23 notes, two double-claimed) against a mechanical parse. `test_real_corpus_is_parseable`
guards the guard: if front-matter parsing ever silently yields nothing, the uniqueness check
would pass vacuously, so a floor of >20 parsed IDs is asserted.
