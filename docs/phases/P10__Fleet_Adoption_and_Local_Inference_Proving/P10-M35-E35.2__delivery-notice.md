# Delivery Notice: P10-M35-E35.2 — Operator's standing brief

## Summary

The operator's standing brief is recorded, as a new system-tier document:
`governance/systems/fleet-operator-brief.md` **v1.0.0**. E35.1 recorded *what the fleet operator is*
and what it may never do; E35.3 recorded *what a blocked instance owes*. Neither said what the
operator needs to **know** in order to do the job on a given cycle. This closes that gap.

The brief is a standing set of **questions and pointers**. It states, for each of five areas, what
the operator must determine and **where to obtain it** — and never the answer. It is consumable by a
chat re-reading it, a daemon loading it on boot, or a person consulting it, and it **carries no
cadence of its own**.

**Nothing was built.** No sweep, no scheduler, no boot loader, no cron entry, no detector. Two
Markdown files changed; no code, no configuration, no tooling. The Hard Constraint held.

**The Authority Boundary, the three duties, the sequencing reading, and the handback rule are cited,
not restated.** No second copy of the limit exists anywhere in this delivery.

## Surface choice and reasoning

**Chosen: a new system-tier document, `governance/systems/fleet-operator-brief.md`,** with
`fleet-operator.md` §Reference pointing to it — the spec's recommended option, taken for the reasons
the spec gave and one the delivery confirmed.

| Reason | How it held up in the writing |
|---|---|
| **Different change cadences.** The role and its boundary should be close to immutable; the brief is expected to gain and lose items as field practice evolves. | Confirmed by the P10-GH-7 material: Area 5 carries a caveat that is explicitly expected to change when the detection problem is solved in P11. In a single file, retiring that caveat would be an edit to the boundary document. |
| **Existing precedent one role over** (`system-hq.md` normative + `system-hq-seed.md` operational). | A reader already knows how to navigate that pair; this delivery reproduces the same shape for the operator, which also makes the *distinctness of the two roles* legible at the file level rather than only in prose. |
| **A briefing item must never be mistaken for a rule.** | Easiest to guarantee across a file boundary. The brief opens by stating it is not normative — *"Where this brief and any of those documents appear to disagree, **those documents win.** This one briefs; it does not govern."* — and `fleet-operator.md`'s back-pointer labels it *"informative, not normative"* from the other side. |

**The rejected alternative** (a delimited section inside `fleet-operator.md`) was viable — the spec
permitted it if the brief were short enough that a second file is overhead. It is not: at ~290 lines
the brief would have roughly doubled `fleet-operator.md` and buried a 235-line normative record
inside an operational one.

**What the back-pointer cost.** `fleet-operator.md` goes **v1.1.0 → v1.2.0** — a minor bump for a
§Reference addition and its changelog row. **No normative text in that document changed**; the diff
is 6 lines (front-matter version, 3 reference lines, changelog row). The `system-hq.md` precedent
treated the equivalent edit as a patch (v1.0.1/v1.0.2); a minor bump was chosen here because this
one *introduces a companion document into the corpus* rather than repairing a one-way cross-reference.
Flagged for the Milestone Chat as a judgment call, not a rule.

## Authorization check — the currency wording, verbatim

DoD: *no line reads as a standing authorization; a reader executing the brief literally performs no
unauthorized write.* Area 4 is where that risk concentrates, because duty 3 is the fleet-wide one.
Quoted in full:

> **How this duty is discharged — and where it stops:**
>
> 1. **Observe.** Determine the gap: which registered projects are behind, and by what.
> 2. **Propose.** Surface the gap to the authority that can authorize the work, as a proposal.
> 3. **Stop there.** Then wait.
>
> **The write is not yours to start.** *"Update every registered project to version X"* is a
> **fleet-wide write**, and it does not execute on a spoken word — **including a word spoken by this
> brief.** Duty 3 is fully bound by the Authority Boundary for exactly this reason
> (`fleet-operator.md` §The three duties, duty 3). A per-project roll-forward proceeds when
> authority for it arrives through the governance chain; determining that it *should* happen is not
> that authority arriving, and neither is this document listing the step.
>
> **The recurring question:** *what is behind, who has been told, and what am I waiting on?* Note
> that all three parts of that question are answerable without performing a single write.

Three properties worth naming for the reviewer:

1. **The verb sequence terminates in a stop.** Observe → propose → *stop there. Then wait.* There is
   no fourth step, and the absence is stated rather than implied.
2. **The brief disqualifies itself as a source of authority.** *"including a word spoken by this
   brief"* is the sentence that closes the loop the spec's Failure mode 2 warned about: a document
   that lists a step can be read as sanctioning it, and this one says in the same paragraph that it
   does not.
3. **The recurring question is write-free by construction.** Every area's closing question is
   answerable by observation alone; that was the drafting test applied to each.

**The same discipline applied to the other fleet-reaching areas:** Area 3 states that sequencing
confers nothing retroactively and that where authority cannot be identified the correct outcome is
that the work **does not run** (cited to §Sequencing Is Not Governance); Area 5 states that the
operator does **not** resolve a handback, supply the missing judgment, or decide whether blocked
work proceeds. Area 1's recurring question — *"where is the authority, and did it arrive through the
governance chain rather than through someone saying so?"* — is the seam applied as a per-act test.

**A reader executing this brief literally performs: reads, lookups, determinations, and one
proposal.** No write in the set.

## What carried over, and what SN-24 retired

The extension note (a first-class section of the brief, not a footnote) records:

- **Carried over — the content-shape.** The seed's sequence **identity → boundary → how work is
  found → what to do first**, which works because it meets the limit before the work. The brief
  keeps it: this is why "Its own boundary" is **Area 1** rather than Area 5 as the spec listed it.
  The spec set no order, and the reordering serves its own stated requirement that the brief *"cannot
  be consumed without meeting the limit."* The reordering is stated in the document ("The Boundary
  Comes First") so it reads as deliberate.
- **Retired — the ritual form.** *"Paste this seed into a new session"* and its intended rhythm.
  Per the HQ Ruling on SN-24, **Decision 1** (*the operator is not a chat, and there is no daily
  re-spawn*) and **Decision 2** (name the **role**, not the implementation). The brief has no spawn
  instruction, no paste step, and no rhythm of its own — it has **consumption modes** instead.
- **Unaffected.** System HQ's own seed (above).

Neither ruling decision was re-litigated. Both are cited as binding.
