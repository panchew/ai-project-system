# Epic E35.3 — Handback + one-level escalation + Creation Chat awareness

## Context

SN-25's founding observation (Creation Chat, 2026-07-30): **an autonomy that cannot hand back is not
autonomy — it is an unattended process that fails silently.** The framework has dispatched work to
run unattended since P7 (`bin/run-dev-agent`), exercised repeatedly through M33 and M34. It has no
recorded way for that work to surface a block it cannot resolve. An instance that hits something
requiring human judgment has exactly two exits today: finish wrongly, or stop silently.

The HQ Ruling on SN-25 (2026-07-30) settled three things this Epic records: the handback's
**destination** (the immediate parent, not "a human"), the **routing rule** (exactly one level), and
**Creation Chat's awareness** (visibility, never authority). None of it is open for re-debate here.

E35.1 (merged, `5ebce92`) recorded the fleet operator's boundary and, correctly, stopped at *"the
correct outcome is that it does not run"* without specifying any route — that route is this Epic's.

## The one thing this Epic must get right

**Do not write the one-level rule a fourth time.**

The rule is normative in PSG §13D, AOG §3.10, and `chat-hierarchy.md` — three surfaces that already
agree. A fourth statement of it, in this Epic's own words, creates a fourth thing that can drift, and
the framework has already paid for one duplication trap (System HQ's Authority Boundary, which now
carries a stated must-always-agree invariant across three files precisely because it was reproduced).

What this Epic adds is **not the rule** but three things the rule does not currently say:

1. **The rule applies to an instance that is not a chat.** PSG §13D speaks of levels and completion
   notices; the escalation-notice template speaks of *"any chat."* An unattended agentic run is
   neither, and nothing today says it must surface anything at all. **That gap is the whole Epic.**
2. **The intervention that follows is authority-bearing.** When a parent resolves a handback, that
   resolution carries authority — it is not advice the blocked instance may weigh.
3. **P9-GH-1 is not closed by any of this.** Adjacent protection against the same authority class,
   not the same fix. It must be named as still open **in the delivered record itself**, not merely in
   this spec.

**Extend by reference; restate nothing.** Where the new text needs the one-level rule, it cites the
surface that already carries it. If the Epic Chat concludes a surface must be *edited* rather than
cited, then **every tier carrying that rule moves consistently in the same commit** — PSG §13D, AOG
§3.10, and `chat-hierarchy.md` — with version bumps and changelog entries on the documents that carry
versions (PSG v2.3.0, AOG v2.10.0). A partial tier update is the failure mode; there is no such
thing as amending this rule in one place.

## Reconciliation duty (Milestone Chat Stage-2 finding on E35.1)

E35.1 delivered, in `fleet-operator.md`'s normative text: *"declining to act is a successful outcome
for this role, not a failure to perform."* That sentence is correct in its own frame — refusing an
unauthorized fleet-wide write is a success. **Read without this Epic's rule, it is also a licence to
stop silently** — the exact failure mode SN-25 exists to eliminate.

**This Epic must close that reading explicitly.** The delivered record must state that declining to
act and surfacing the block are **both required**, not alternatives: an instance that stops without
handing back has performed half the obligation. Do this by naming the interaction directly — a
cross-reference from the handback rule to `fleet-operator.md`'s outcome sentence, or an added
sentence in `fleet-operator.md` binding its "does not run" outcome to the surfacing obligation, is
acceptable; leaving the tension for a future reader to notice is not.

If amending `fleet-operator.md`: it is v1.0.0 with a changelog table — bump it and add a row.

## Goals and Scope

Record, normatively, that a blocked autonomous instance must hand back; that the handback goes to its
immediate parent and nowhere else; and that Creation Chat's awareness of escalations is visibility
only.

**In scope:**
- The **handback rule**, including the authority-bearing property and the destination.
- The **one-level rule's application** to handback, by reference, plus the explicit P9-GH-1
  non-closure and the CFO-is-not-a-level clarification.
- **Creation Chat awareness**, recorded as visibility-only with its single outlet named.
- The **P10-GH-7 caveat**, attached to the handback rule where a reader of that rule will meet it.
- The **E35.1 reconciliation** above.

**Out of scope — do not touch:**
- **Any mechanism** (Hard Constraint). No block detector, no runner→chat channel, no notification,
  no dispatch wiring. **This is the epic most likely to drift into building** — the rule you are
  writing describes a signal that does not exist yet, and writing the detector is P11's (Drivr).
- **Solving P10-GH-7.** In scope to state honestly; out of scope to fix.
- **Closing P9-GH-1.** Explicitly not closed. Do not imply otherwise anywhere.
- **Amending Creation Chat Seed Rule 3.** It stands as written; restate it, do not rewrite it.
- **The escalation notice's existing schema.** No new artifact type and no new authority model — SN-25
  is explicit on both. Extending the template's *applicability* text is in scope; changing its
  required fields is not.
- **E35.2's standing brief and E35.4's execution matrix.** Sibling territory.
- `.ai-project.yml`, `model-routing-policy.md`.

## Surface decision (recommended, not fixed)

**Recommended primary surface: `governance/systems/chat-hierarchy.md`, adjacent to its Execution Mode
section.**

The handback obligation attaches to **agentic Execution Mode** — the condition of running unattended —
and Execution Mode is defined and made normative in exactly that section (P9-M31-E31.1). A rule about
what an unattended instance owes belongs where "unattended instance" is defined. That section also
already records its own gap honestly (*"no dispatch mechanism yet consumes a Phase/Milestone agentic
declaration"*), which is the register P10-GH-7's caveat needs.

**Secondary surface: `governance/templates/escalation-notice.md`** — extend its applicability
statement so *"any chat"* also reaches an autonomous instance, and add the handback trigger to its
§Trigger list. It is the carrier artifact; leaving it describing chats only would make the new rule
point at a template that excludes its own subject.

**For Creation Chat awareness:** `governance/systems/creation-chat-guide.md` is the natural home;
`governance/templates/seed.md` Rule 3 is **restated, not amended**.

**PSG §13D / AOG §3.10:** recommended **untouched** — they carry the one-level rule, which this Epic
does not change. If the Epic Chat judges a normative-tier anchor necessary, see the all-tiers-together
constraint above.

Whichever surfaces are chosen, record the choice and reasoning in the Delivery Notice.

## Deliverables

1. **The handback rule, recorded normatively.** A blocked autonomous instance — one that has
   encountered something requiring judgment it cannot supply — **MUST** surface the block, with
   enough context for the receiving level to act on it: the nature of the blocker, what was
   attempted, and what it could not resolve. It travels as an **escalation notice** — the existing
   artifact type, no new type, no new authority model.
2. **The destination, stated exactly.** The **immediate parent** — not "a human." An instance has no
   way to identify or select a human directly. The human is reached because the chain **terminates at
   a manual level by construction**: Creation and HQ are manual-only, permanently (SN-22), so every
   escalation that keeps escalating arrives at a human in a bounded number of hops. **Termination is
   guaranteed by SN-22, not by hope** — write that reasoning, not just the conclusion.
3. **The authority-bearing property.** The intervention that resolves a handback carries authority;
   it is not advisory input the blocked instance may weigh and discard.
4. **The one-level rule applied, by reference** (see "The one thing this Epic must get right"),
   including:
   - the parent decides **resolve-or-escalate**; no instance names a target above its parent;
   - **P9-GH-1 is not closed by this** — named as still open in the delivered record itself, with the
     reason (adjacent protection against the same authority class, not the same fix);
   - **the CFO is not a level in the chain** and may answer at any point without that being a bypass;
     the obligation a direct answer creates is **recording** — the decision must land where the level
     that would otherwise have ruled can see it. Cite M34's worked examples: the two committed
     escalation notices (Milestone Chat could not open → Phase Chat diagnosed and issued its own
     notice to HQ → HQ ruled: two hops, no level skipped), and the `fieldledger-assesment` direct
     CFO resolution as the recording-obligation example.
5. **Creation Chat awareness, visibility-only.** Aware of all escalation notices wherever they arise —
   a **retrieval** property over committed artifacts (a re-instantiated Creation Chat reads the
   directory), **never a subscription and never a seat**. Seed Rule 3 restated: the Creation Chat
   holds no governance authority. Its **one legitimate outlet** named: issuing a **steering note to
   HQ** — direction-setting, not resolution — which is how SN-23, SN-24, and SN-25 themselves arrived.
   State plainly that awareness must never become a resolution path.
6. **The P10-GH-7 caveat, attached where it will be read.** Block detection is measured broken in
   **both** directions — E33.2 Run A returned **exit 0 having done zero work**; E33.4 returned **exit
   2 having produced complete, green work** — corroborated across two projects, and the lane that
   would supply a trustworthy signal (`epic_qa`) has **zero captured runs** (G11). A reader of the
   handback rule must meet this dependency in the same reading, not two documents away. It is
   recorded, **not** solved.
7. **The E35.1 reconciliation** per "Reconciliation duty" above.

## Hard Constraint (carried from the Milestone spec)

**Nothing built in P10.** Every deliverable of this Epic is a governance **record**. This Epic carries
the highest drift risk in M35: it describes a signal (a detected block) that **no working mechanism
produces**, and the temptation to make the rule "real" by building the detector is exactly the trap.
If this Epic finds itself writing code that detects a block, opens a channel, sends a notification, or
switches a mode, it has drifted out of M35's scope and must **stop and escalate to the Milestone
Chat** rather than proceed.

Recording an obligation whose enforcement mechanism does not exist yet is **the intended outcome**,
not a shortfall — P11 builds against these rules, which is why they must be written correctly first.

## Notes

- **The destination correction is the substance of Decision 1.** SN-25 originally read "hand back to a
  human"; HQ corrected it to the immediate parent. An instance cannot identify a human — writing "a
  human" would have produced a rule no instance can execute. Preserve the correction *and* its
  reasoning; a reader who sees only "immediate parent" will eventually re-derive "but the human never
  hears about it," and the SN-22 termination argument is the answer to that.
- **Instance-judged routing was considered and rejected**, and the record should say why: it lets a
  child choose its own judge. An Epic routing straight to HQ steps around its Milestone's Stage-2
  authority, and the parent may never learn its own epic is blocked.
- **Two adjacent things share a name and are not the same fix.** P9-GH-1 is the merge-authorization
  hole at Milestone→Phase and Phase→HQ. The one-level rule is about where an escalation is *addressed*.
  Both concern authority travelling between the same levels, which is exactly why a future reader will
  conflate them unless the record refuses to.
