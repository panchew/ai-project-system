# Delivery Notice: P10-M35-E35.3 — Handback + one-level escalation + Creation Chat awareness

## Summary

The rule that closes the silent-failure exit is recorded. A blocked autonomous instance **MUST**
surface the block; it goes to the instance's **immediate parent**; it travels as the **existing**
escalation notice; and the intervention that resolves it **carries authority**.

**Nothing was built.** No block detector, no runner→chat channel, no notification, no mode switch,
no dispatch wiring. Four Markdown governance documents changed; no code, no configuration, no
tooling. The Hard Constraint held, and the epic's stated highest-drift risk — making the rule
"real" by building the detector — did not materialize.

The one-level escalation rule was **cited, not restated**. No fourth normative copy exists.

---

## The cite-vs-edit decision on the one-level rule

**Decision: CITE. No surface carrying the one-level rule was edited.**

`governance/PROJECT-SYSTEM-GUIDELINES.md` §13D, `governance/AI-OPERATING-GUIDELINES.md` §3.10, and
`governance/systems/chat-hierarchy.md` §Communication Protocol → *"Upward — 1-to-1, one level at a
time"* are **all three untouched by this delivery**. PSG stays at v2.3.0 and AOG at v2.10.0; no
version bump or changelog row was required on either, because neither changed.

**Reasoning.** All three were read in their own files before deciding (Prerequisite 3 below), and
they agree in substance: escalations travel up one level, every level has exactly one parent, the
receiving level decides absorb-or-escalate, no level skips its parent to reach a grandparent. This
Epic does not change any of that. What it adds is a **new subject** for the existing rule — an
instance that is not a chat — and a **new occasion** for it, the handback. Neither is an amendment
to the rule; both are applications of it. Editing a normative surface to record an application
would have created a fourth thing that can drift, against a corpus that has already paid for one
duplication trap (System HQ's Authority Boundary, which now carries an explicit must-always-agree
invariant across three files precisely because it was reproduced).

**Consequence: the all-tiers-together constraint was never triggered.** Because nothing was edited,
there is no partial tier update to prove against — the failure mode the spec named cannot have
occurred. The delivered text cites the rule in a three-row table naming each surface and what it
carries, then states only the application beneath it.

## Surface choices and reasoning

Four surfaces, all as the spec recommended. No surface was chosen outside its recommendation, and
none was added.

### 1. Primary — `governance/systems/chat-hierarchy.md`

New `###` section, **"Handback: what a blocked agentic instance owes"**, placed *inside* the
**Execution Mode: Manual vs. Agentic** section, as its final subsection (after "Manual Chat Model
Verification", before `## Level 0`).

**Reasoning.** The obligation attaches to **agentic Execution Mode** — the condition of running
unattended — and that condition is defined and made normative in exactly that section
(P9-M31-E31.1). A rule about what an unattended instance owes belongs where "unattended instance"
is defined, not in a section a reader reaches by a second hop. Placement inside the section also
puts the rule two screens below the SN-22 manual-only statement it depends on ("Creation Chat and
HQ Chat: Manual-Only, Permanently"), so the termination argument cites a fact the reader has
already met. That section additionally records its own gap honestly (*"no dispatch mechanism yet
consumes a Phase/Milestone agentic declaration"*), which is the register P10-GH-7's caveat needed
to sit in without reading as an aberration.

**Convention followed:** inline dated note — `*(Added P10-M35-E35.3, 2026-07-30, recording the HQ
Ruling on SN-25, Decisions 1–2 and 7.)*` — per that document's own established precedent. **No
`version` field and no `## Changelog` was added**, per P10-GH-8 and the Milestone Chat's
carry-forward note upholding E35.1's identical refusal.

### 2. Secondary — `governance/templates/escalation-notice.md`

Applicability statement extended; handback added to the `## Trigger` guidance; `last_updated`
2026-06-17 → 2026-07-30.

**Reasoning.** It is the carrier artifact. Leaving it framed as *"any chat"* would have left the
new rule pointing at a template that excludes its own subject. The delivered text says so
directly: *"Read 'any chat' throughout this template as **any instance of any level, attended or
unattended**."*

**Required schema fields are unchanged** — `type`, `milestone`, `issued_by`, `issued_to`, `date`,
`status`, all still exactly as committed, and the template now states that explicitly: *"This
template's schema is unchanged by it; a handback needs no new field."* No new artifact type and no
new authority model was introduced.

### 3. Creation Chat awareness — `governance/systems/creation-chat-guide.md`

New `##` section, **"Escalation Awareness — Visibility Only"**, placed after "When to Expect a
Progress Digest" and before "CFO PR Review Gate".

**Reasoning.** Both neighbours are about what the Creation Chat *sees*, and the section's single
sanctioned outlet is the Steering Note channel already documented two sections above ("When to
Write a Steering Note"), so the outlet is a cross-reference rather than a new mechanism. This file
also carries neither front-matter nor a changelog, so it took the same inline dated note as
`chat-hierarchy.md` — consistent with P10-GH-8's deferral rather than deciding the convention
under cover of this edit.

**`governance/templates/seed.md` was not touched.** Rule 3 is quoted verbatim as a blockquote in
the new section and labelled as restated. Amending it was out of scope and it is byte-identical on
this branch.

### 4. Reconciliation — `governance/systems/fleet-operator.md`

Amended in place, **v1.0.0 → v1.1.0**, with a changelog row.

**Reasoning.** The spec offered two acceptable forms (a cross-reference from the handback rule, or
an added sentence in `fleet-operator.md`). **Both were delivered**, because the tension is
two-sided: a reader arriving at either document must not be able to reach the wrong conclusion from
it alone. `fleet-operator.md` is versioned with a changelog table, so unlike the two unversioned
documents it took a proper bump and row.

Bound from **both** ends, so neither document can be read alone to the wrong conclusion.

**In `governance/systems/fleet-operator.md`** (§The seam, and why it is load-bearing —
immediately after the *"declining to act is a successful outcome"* sentence it reconciles). The
binding sentence, quoted:

> **"Does not run" is not "stops silently."** *(Added P10-M35-E35.3, 2026-07-30.)* Declining to
> act and **surfacing the block** are **both** required, and they are one sequence rather than two
> options to choose between: decline, **then** surface. An instance that stops without handing
> back has performed half the obligation, and the half it skipped is the one that lets authority
> reach the problem at all.

Followed by the no-widening guard, quoted:

> Nothing in the Authority Boundary above is widened or narrowed by this. The successful outcome
> is still that the unauthorized act **does not execute**; what is added is that its non-execution
> must be **visible** to the level that can supply what was missing.

**In `governance/systems/chat-hierarchy.md`** (subsection "Declining to act and surfacing the
block are both required"), quoted:

> **It is not a licence to stop silently.** Declining to act and surfacing the block are **both**
> required, and they are one sequence rather than two options: decline, **then** surface. An
> instance that stops without handing back has performed half the obligation — and the half it
> skipped is the one this section exists to impose.

`fleet-operator.md` is bumped to **v1.1.0** with a changelog row recording exactly this, and its
