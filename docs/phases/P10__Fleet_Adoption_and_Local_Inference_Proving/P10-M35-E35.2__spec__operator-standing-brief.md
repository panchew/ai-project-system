# Epic E35.2 — Operator's standing brief

## Context

E35.1 (merged, `5ebce92`) recorded **what** the fleet operator is and what it may never do.
E35.3 (merged, `8324f16`) recorded what a blocked instance owes. Neither says what the operator needs
to **know** in order to do the job on any given cycle — which projects it is responsible for, what
state they are in, what occupies the lane, and where to find each of those things.

SN-22 named that gap: the CFO had no recorded, repeatable way to bring the machine-level participant
up to speed. P9-M32-E32.2 answered it for **System HQ** with
`governance/systems/system-hq-seed.md` — a self-contained prompt carrying identity, authority
boundary, seven rules of engagement, and a "What to Do Right Now" step.

**SN-24 then retired the form that answer was framed in.** M35's original scoping called for a *daily
re-instantiation ritual* — which presumes a chat re-spawning itself each morning, exactly the
chat-shaped assumption the HQ Ruling on SN-24 struck out. What survives is the **content**: a
**standing brief**, form-neutral, that a chat can re-read, a daemon can load on boot, or a person can
consult — on whatever cadence the filler happens to have, including none.

## The one thing this Epic must get right

**A standing brief is a standing set of questions and pointers — not a status dashboard, and not a
standing authorization.** Two failure modes, both of which produce a document that looks right on
delivery day and is harmful three weeks later.

**Failure mode 1: it rots.** The tempting brief enumerates current fleet state — *"six projects, all
at v7.0.0; `home_finance` and `local-agent-runner` are the proving pair."* That is true today and
false soon, and nothing in this repository updates it. Worse, the one duty most damaged by a stale
brief is precisely **governance-version currency**: an operator reading "all projects are current"
performs no check at all. **The brief must state what the operator needs to know and where to obtain
it — never a snapshot of the answer.** Where fleet state is genuinely machine-local (enrollment, live
versions, what is in the lane), the brief points at the source and says plainly that this repository
is not it.

**Failure mode 2: it authorizes.** A brief that says *"each cycle, bring every project behind the
current version up to it"* has manufactured, as a standing instruction, exactly the authority
`fleet-operator.md`'s seam denies — a fleet-wide write executing without authority arriving. **A
standing brief must never become a standing authorization.** Currency duties are phrased as
**observe and propose**, never as **perform**: the operator determines what is behind and surfaces
it; the write happens when authority arrives, not because the brief said so.

Both failure modes have the same tell: a sentence in the brief that would still be executed by an
operator who read nothing else. If a line reads as a standing order to write, rewrite it as a
question to answer.

## Goals and Scope

Produce the operator's standing brief: form-neutral, non-rotting, non-authorizing, and usable by a
reader who knows nothing about what implements the role.

**In scope:**
- The **standing brief** itself — what the operator needs each cycle, within E35.1's boundary.
- The **extension note** connecting it to M32/E32.2's re-instantiation seed, and what changed (form,
  not content — SN-24).
- **Form-neutral consumption**: usable by re-reading, by loading on boot, or by consulting.

**Out of scope — do not touch:**
- **Any mechanism** (Hard Constraint). No sweep, no scheduler, no boot loader, no cron entry. The
  brief is read by whatever fills the role; nothing here builds the reader.
- **`governance/systems/system-hq-seed.md` and `system-hq.md`'s "Daily re-instantiation seed"
  reference label.** Those belong to **System HQ**, a *distinct role* (E35.1). SN-24 retired the
  ritual framing for **the operator's brief**, not for System HQ's own seed. Leave both alone — and
  do not merge the two roles back together, which is the whole point E35.1 spent a document making.
- **Re-stating the Authority Boundary or the three duties.** They are normative in
  `fleet-operator.md` v1.1.0; cite them. (Same discipline E35.3 applied to the one-level rule, and for
  the same reason.)
- **The handback rule.** Normative in `chat-hierarchy.md` (E35.3); the brief may point at it, and
  must not restate or vary it.
- **Fleet state itself.** Do not enumerate enrolled projects, their versions, or the lane's contents
  anywhere in the brief. See Failure mode 1.
- **E35.4's execution matrix and E35.5's evaluation.** Sibling territory.
- `.ai-project.yml`, `model-routing-policy.md`, PSG, AOG.

## Surface decision (recommended, not fixed)

**Recommended: a new system-tier document, `governance/systems/fleet-operator-brief.md`,** with
`fleet-operator.md` §Reference pointing to it and the brief citing the boundary rather than repeating
it.

Reasons: the two documents have **different change cadences** — the role and its boundary should be
close to immutable, while the brief is expected to gain and lose items as the fleet's practice
evolves; keeping them in one file makes every operational tweak a boundary-document edit. It also
matches the existing precedent one role over (`system-hq.md` normative + `system-hq-seed.md`
operational), which a reader already knows how to navigate.

**Also acceptable:** a clearly delimited section inside `fleet-operator.md`, if the Epic Chat judges
the brief short enough that a second file is overhead. If so, it must be unmistakably separated from
the normative text — the brief is **not** normative in the way the Authority Boundary is, and a
reader must never take a briefing item for a rule.

Record the choice and reasoning in the Delivery Notice.

## Deliverables

1. **The standing brief.** What the operator needs each cycle to run the lane and keep the fleet
   current, within E35.1's boundary. It must cover, at minimum:
   - **Scope of responsibility** — how the operator determines which projects it is responsible for,
     and where that answer lives (machine-local; not in this repository).
   - **Lane state** — how it determines what is running, what is queued, and that the
     one-reasoning-job-at-a-instant concurrency rule is being honoured.
   - **Governance-version currency** — how it determines which registered projects are behind the
     current framework version, phrased as **observe and propose**, never as perform.
   - **Blocked work** — how it learns that something has handed back, with the P10-GH-7 caveat
     attached or cited so a reader does not assume this signal is reliable.
   - **Its own boundary** — a pointer to `fleet-operator.md`'s Authority Boundary, with the
     no-authority-on-speech seam named, so the brief cannot be consumed without meeting the limit.
2. **Form-neutral consumption, stated explicitly** — the brief is consumable by re-reading, by
   loading on boot, or by human consultation, and **carries no cadence of its own**. "Each cycle"
   means each time the filler does the work, whatever its cadence; the brief neither requires a daily
   run nor prohibits one.
3. **The extension note** — an explicit statement connecting the brief to M32/E32.2's
   re-instantiation seed: what carried over (the content-shape: identity, boundary, how work is
   found, what to do first) and what did not (the ritual form — SN-24 Decision 1: *the operator is
   not a chat, and there is no daily re-spawn*). State that System HQ's seed is a **different role's**
   artifact and is unaffected.

## Notes

- **The seed's shape is a good teacher and a bad template.** `system-hq-seed.md` is worth reading for
  how it sequences identity → boundary → rules → how work is found → what to do first. But it ends in
  *"paste this seed into a new session"*, which is the ritual form SN-24 retired for this role. Take
  the sequence; leave the paste.
- **"Each cycle" is deliberately not "each day."** A daemon's cycle is a boot; a person's is whenever
  they sit down; a chat's is whenever it is re-read. The brief describes what is needed per cycle
  without owning the clock — that is what form-neutral means here, and it is the whole of SN-24's
  correction.
- **This is the epic where the fleet's real state is most tempting to write down, and it is exactly
  the thing to leave out.** The repository does not know it, cannot keep it, and would mislead anyone
  who trusted it. Point at the source and move on.
