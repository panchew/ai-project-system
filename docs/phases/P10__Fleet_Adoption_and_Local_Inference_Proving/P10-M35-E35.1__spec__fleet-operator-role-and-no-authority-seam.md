# Epic E35.1 — Fleet-operator role + no-authority-on-speech seam

## Context

The framework has a machine-wide participant already: **System HQ**, canonized in P9-M32-E32.1
(`governance/systems/system-hq.md` v1.0.1) from field practice adopted 2026-07-16 (SN-21). It is a
**request-executing desk** — a project files a `system_request`, System HQ acts within its ordinary
tool authority, answers with a `system_response`, and escalates anything review-, merge-, or
scope-shaped. It never initiates.

SN-23 then described something System HQ is not: a **fleet operator** that runs the serialized
local-inference lane, decides what runs next, and keeps registered projects current on governance
version. During M33 and M34 the CFO *was* that operator, by hand, with no governance record saying
what the role may or may never do. SN-24 (2026-07-28) corrected the one thing M35's original scoping
got wrong about it — the operator was described as a chat ("System Chat"), and the HQ Ruling on
SN-24 amended M35 to name the operator **by role, not by implementation**: a chat window, a daemon,
a cron job, or a person with a terminal are all admissible fillers, and Drivr (P11) is the *expected*
filler, not a dependency.

This Epic writes that role down. It is the base the rest of M35 stands on: E35.2's standing brief
operates *within* this Epic's authority boundary, and E35.3's handback rule presupposes an operator
whose authority is already bounded.

## The one thing this Epic must get right

**Recording the fleet-operator role must not read as expanding System HQ.**

Four live sentences in the committed corpus pin against a "mighty governing System Chat" (all four
quoted in Grounding above). This Epic records a role that operates a lane and keeps projects
current — which, read carelessly, is exactly the thing those sentences pin against. The difference
is real and must be *written*, not assumed:

| | System HQ (existing) | Fleet operator (this Epic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Posture** | Reactive — acts on a filed `system_request` | Initiative-bearing, in a narrow, non-governance sense — keeps a lane busy |
| **Unit of work** | One request, answered with one `system_response` | One serialized lane, run continuously |
| **What it decides** | Nothing (execute, never decide) | **Sequencing only** — which already-authorized work occupies the lane next |
| **Authority over governance acts** | None — review/merge/scope ⇒ `status: escalated` | None — identical; the seam below is its counterpart |

**The two roles are distinct; the same filler may hold both.** The operator role is *not* an
expansion of System HQ's authority boundary and must not be written as one. If the Epic Chat finds
itself needing to amend `system-hq.md`'s Out-of-Scope pin or the seed's Rule 6 in order to make the
operator record fit, **that is the signal it has drifted** — stop and escalate to the Milestone
Chat rather than widening a pinned boundary.

**The distinction that carries the weight: sequencing is not governance.** "Decides what runs next"
must be written so it cannot be read as "decides." The operator chooses *which already-authorized
unit of work occupies the lane next*, and nothing else. Review, acceptance, merge authorization, and
scope changes are never the operator's — the same acts System HQ must escalate, for the same reason.

**If the Authority Boundary block is touched at all, both copies move word-for-word in the same
commit.** This Epic has no reason to touch it (see above), but the trap is live: a one-word edit in
`system-hq.md` that does not land identically in `chat-hierarchy.md`'s annex breaks a
stated-must-always-agree invariant. Say plainly in the Delivery Notice whether it was touched.

## Goals and Scope

Record the fleet-operator role and its authority boundary normatively, form-neutral, so a reader can
state what the operator may and may never do without finding any implementation named.

**In scope:**
- The operator's three duties, recorded normatively: **operate** the serialized local-inference lane;
  **sequence** what runs next within it; **keep registered projects current** on governance version.
- The **no-authority-on-speech seam**, recorded normatively.
- The **form-neutrality** statement, with the single permitted Drivr reference.
- The **System HQ relationship** stated explicitly (distinct role, non-expanding, same filler
  admissible).
- Cross-reference hygiene on whichever surface is chosen, both directions.

**Out of scope — do not touch:**
- **Any mechanism.** No scheduler, no lane daemon, no sweep, no dispatch wiring (Hard Constraint).
- **Any authority expansion**, anywhere in the corpus. This Epic's whole point is a bounded role.
- **System HQ's Authority Boundary text** and the `system_request`/`system_response` schemas — not
  this Epic's business.
- **The handback rule, one-level escalation, and Creation Chat awareness** — E35.3's deliverables.
  E35.1 records the *boundary*; E35.3 records what a blocked instance does. Do not pre-empt it.
- **The standing brief's content** — E35.2's deliverable. E35.1 defines the boundary the brief
  operates within; it does not write the brief.
- **The execution matrix** — E35.4's deliverable, including any statement about running modes.
- `.ai-project.yml`, `model-routing-policy.md`, and every other config or policy surface. This Epic
  changes no configuration.

## Surface decision (recommended, not fixed)

The Milestone spec leaves the governance surface to the Epic Chat. The Milestone Chat's
recommendation, with its reasons, so the Epic Chat does not have to re-derive them:

**Recommended: a new system-tier document, `governance/systems/fleet-operator.md`.**

1. It keeps the role's normative text **out of** `system-hq.md`, where any addition is read against a
   pin that expansion is out of scope — the cleanest way to satisfy "the one thing this Epic must get
   right."
2. `system-hq.md` is a document about a *participant whose form is already fixed* (a desk that
   exchanges two artifact types). The operator is deliberately **form-neutral** — its own document can
   say so without qualifying a neighbouring document's fixed-form claims.
3. `chat-hierarchy.md` is the four-level *per-project* hierarchy plus an out-of-hierarchy annex; the
   operator is neither a level nor a per-project participant. A third annex there would crowd a
   document whose organizing idea is the vertical.

**Also acceptable**, if the Epic Chat judges it cleaner: an operator-role section inside
`chat-hierarchy.md` alongside the System HQ annex. **Not acceptable:** writing the role into
`system-hq.md` as new System HQ duties — that *is* the expansion the corpus pins against.

Whichever is chosen, **document the reasoning in the Delivery Notice**, and make the cross-references
two-way: the new/extended surface points at `system-hq.md` (for the distinction) and
`chat-hierarchy.md`; `system-hq.md` §Reference and `chat-hierarchy.md` §Reference gain a pointer
back. A one-way reference is the defect E32.2 left behind and E32.1's changelog v1.0.1 had to fix
afterwards — do not repeat it.

## Deliverables

1. **The operator role, recorded normatively and form-neutral** — the three duties (operate the
   serialized local-inference lane; sequence what runs next; keep registered projects current on
   governance version), in role-only language.
2. **The no-authority-on-speech seam, recorded normatively** — the operator holds **no authority to
   act fleet-wide on a spoken word**; a request to it is a **proposal until it carries authority**
   behind it. Includes the generalization SN-24 recorded: a daemon has no speech at all, only gates,
   which makes the seam *stronger* under that form, not weaker.
3. **The sequencing-is-not-governance statement** — what "decides what runs next" does and does not
   mean, naming review, acceptance, merge authorization, and scope change as never the operator's.
4. **The form-neutrality statement** — a chat window, a daemon, a cron job, or a person with a
   terminal are all admissible fillers; the role and its boundary hold regardless. **Drivr (P11) is
   referenced exactly once**, as the expected filler, explicitly not a dependency (HQ Ruling on
   SN-24, Decision 2).
5. **The System HQ relationship** — stated explicitly: distinct role, not an expansion of System HQ's
   authority, same filler admissible for both.
6. **Cross-reference hygiene** — two-way pointers per the Surface decision above; system-tier
   front-matter and a `## Changelog` entry on every system document created or modified.

## Notes

- **The seam is the reason this Epic exists, not a footnote to it.** "Update every project to v7.0.0"
  is a fleet-wide write. The whole framework exists to stop that class of act from running on a
  spoken word one level up; recording an operator without the seam would recreate it one level down.
- **The operator role is not new authority — it is a name for what the CFO already does by hand.**
  M33 and M34 ran with the CFO as the lane. This Epic writes down the role that was already being
  performed, with its boundary made explicit for the first time.
- **Drivr gets exactly one mention.** HQ Ruling on SN-24, Decision 2: governance names the role; P11
  names the thing that runs it. More than one reference starts making the record depend on a project
  that does not exist yet.
