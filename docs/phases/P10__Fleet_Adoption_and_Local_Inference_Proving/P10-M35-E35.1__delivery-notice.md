# Delivery Notice: P10-M35-E35.1 — Fleet-operator role + no-authority-on-speech seam

## Summary

The fleet-operator role is written down. It is recorded **normatively**, in **role-only
language**, in a new system-tier document — `governance/systems/fleet-operator.md` v1.0.0 —
with its authority boundary explicit for the first time and the **no-authority-on-speech seam**
carried as normative text rather than commentary.

**This Epic recorded a role; it granted nothing.** The role was already being performed: the
Layer-8/CFO was the lane by hand through M33 and M34, with nothing in the corpus saying what
that role may or may never do. Every authority held by every participant is exactly what it was
before this delivery. No mechanism was built (Hard Constraint), and no sibling Epic's territory
was written.

---

## Surface Choice and Reasoning

**Chosen: a new system-tier document, `governance/systems/fleet-operator.md`** — the Milestone
Chat's recommendation, adopted after independently checking its reasons against the committed
corpus rather than accepting them on the spec's word.

Why this surface, and not the two alternatives:

1. **It keeps the role's normative text out of `system-hq.md`.** That document carries a live
   pin that expansion of System HQ's authority is out of scope. Any addition there — however
   carefully worded — is read against that pin, and the reader has to be *argued* out of the
   expansion reading. A separate document means the distinction is structural: two documents,
   two boundaries, neither claiming the other's authority. This is the cleanest way to satisfy
   "the one thing this Epic must get right."
2. **`system-hq.md` describes a participant whose form is fixed** — a desk exchanging two
   artifact types. The operator is deliberately **form-neutral**, and it can say so in its own
   document without qualifying a neighbouring document's fixed-form claims.
3. **`chat-hierarchy.md` is organized around the per-project vertical** (Levels 0–4) plus one
   out-of-hierarchy annex. The operator is neither a level nor a per-project participant; a
   second full annex would have crowded a document whose organizing idea is the vertical. The
   role is *pointed to* from there (a short annex note plus a §Reference entry) rather than
   *housed* there — the reader who arrives via the hierarchy learns the role exists and where
   its boundary is normative, without the hierarchy document absorbing it.

**Rejected, as the spec directs:** writing the role into `system-hq.md` as new System HQ duties.
That *is* the expansion the corpus pins against.

## The Four Pins — Verified Unamended and Uncontradicted

Each pin was re-read **in its committed file** on `epic/P10-M35-E35.1`, not from the spec's
quotation of it, as the starter's Prerequisite Verification step 4 requires. All four stand
unamended, and nothing in the delivered text contradicts them.

reproduced in `chat-hierarchy.md` and `system-hq-seed.md`):

> Cross-Project Participant" annex; the two must always agree word-for-word. If they ever
> diverge, this document is authoritative."

*Status:* **Unamended and satisfied** — see the hash check above. The new document's own
boundary is explicitly marked as stated *here only* and not reproduced elsewhere, so no second
must-always-agree invariant is created.

**Pin 2 — `system-hq.md` §Out of Scope:**

> "**Any expansion of System HQ's authority** beyond the boundary above, or design toward a
> "mighty governing System Chat" — pinned out of scope (SN-21/SN-22)."

*Status:* **Unamended** (the §Out of Scope section is byte-unchanged; only §Reference,
front-matter `version`, and the changelog were edited). **Uncontradicted** — `fleet-operator.md`
adds no authority to System HQ, states in its Purpose that it "deliberately does not design
toward a 'mighty governing System Chat' — that remains pinned out of scope (SN-21/SN-22), as
does any expansion of System HQ's authority", and repeats the point in its own §Out of Scope.
The `system-hq.md` back-pointer added by this Epic names the operator as "a distinct role, not
an expansion of this one" in the pointer text itself, so a reader cannot pick up the reference
without picking up the disclaimer.

**Pin 2b — the same section's deferral** (checked because a lane is adjacent to a sweep):

> "**A scheduled request-sweep / SLA mechanism.** Pickup is on-demand today; a cron/daemon
> sweep with SLA expectations is a future concern, deferred."

*Status:* **Unamended and uncontradicted.** `fleet-operator.md` builds nothing and requires
nothing to be built; its §Out of Scope names "no scheduler, no lane daemon, no request sweep,
no dispatch wiring, no detector of any kind." The operator's lane is not System HQ's pickup
path, and this document does not schedule System HQ's.

**Pin 3 — `system-hq-seed.md` Rule 6 (second person):**

> "You are a request-executing desk. You are **not** being grown toward a "mighty governing
> System Chat," and expansion in that direction is pinned out of scope (SN-21/SN-22). … Pickup
> is on-demand; no daemon."

*Status:* **Unamended** (file untouched) and **uncontradicted** — the operator role is recorded
as a *distinct* role, explicitly not an expansion of the desk. The delivered text states that a
party holding both roles "has exactly the union of two bounded roles, which is still bounded,
and gains nothing that neither role held alone", which is the precise reading Rule 6 protects.

**Pin 4 — P10 phase spec §Out of Scope:**

> "**The "mighty" governing System Chat / fleet-wide write authority.** Pinned vision item. M35
> canonizes the operator role **with the no-authority-on-speech seam intact** — no expansion of
> authority."

*Status:* **Unamended** (phase spec untouched by this Epic) and **satisfied** — the seam is
delivered as the document's normative Authority Boundary, not as a caveat attached to it, and
the fleet-wide duty (governance-version currency) is written with the seam bound to it in the
same breath: *"'update every registered project to version X' is a fleet-wide write, and the
operator **MUST NOT** perform it on a spoken word."*

**No sentence in the delivered record widens any authority held by any participant.** The
delivered text grants nothing, and the only two verbs it attaches to the operator that are not
already bounded — *operate* and *sequence* — are each explicitly bounded in the same section
that introduces them.

### Note on `chat-hierarchy.md`'s front-matter and changelog (transparency)

`governance/systems/fleet-operator.md` (created) carries full system-tier front-matter
(`type` / `status` / `effective_date` / `version: 1.0.0`) and a `## Changelog` table modelled on
`system-hq.md`. `system-hq.md` (modified) was bumped **v1.0.1 → v1.0.2** with a changelog row.

`chat-hierarchy.md` (modified) carries system-tier front-matter but **no `version` field and no
`## Changelog` table** — and it did not before this Epic either. That is the document's own
established convention, shared with 9 of the 15 documents in `governance/systems/`; the versioned
subset is `system-hq.md`, `system-hq-seed.md`, `artifact-communication-protocol.md`,
`bugfix-epic-workflow.md`, and `roles-authorization-team-governance.md`.

**Judgment made, and why:** retrofitting a `version` and a retroactive changelog onto
`chat-hierarchy.md` would have been a corpus-wide convention change smuggled in under a
cross-reference edit — exactly the kind of unrequested widening this Epic is supposed to avoid,
and it would have invented a version number for a document that has never had one. Instead the
change follows **that document's own precedent**: P9-M32-E32.1 added the entire System HQ annex
to it and recorded provenance as an inline italic note (`*(Added P9-M32-E32.1, 2026-07-20, …)*`).
This Epic's annex note is marked the same way — `*(Added P10-M35-E35.1, 2026-07-30.)*` — so the
edit is dated and attributed in the document itself.

**Flagged for the Milestone Chat** as a small, deliberate deviation from the DoD item's literal
wording, made in favour of the DoD item's evident intent (no undocumented edits). If the
Milestone Chat prefers the literal reading, versioning `chat-hierarchy.md` is a one-commit
follow-up — but it is a convention decision above this Epic's authority, so it was not taken
unilaterally.
