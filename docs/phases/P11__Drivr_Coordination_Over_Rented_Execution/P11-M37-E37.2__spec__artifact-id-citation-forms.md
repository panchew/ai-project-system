## Problem Statement

**A shorthand in the normative tier resolves to more than one artifact.**

This is **not** an ID collision. The `GH-` namespace held, and `rulings/` and `escalation-notices/`
allocate no ID at all (E36.5's audit established both). It is a **citation-form** failure, with
SN-23's exact reader-level consequence: a reader following a citation lands on the wrong artifact, or
cannot tell which of two they landed on.

**Three concrete defects, all verified:**

1. **The bare `GH-10` at `PROJECT-SYSTEM-GUIDELINES.md:605`** is the **sole** namespace-stripped
   `GH-<n>` in the corpus, sitting in **the framework's highest-authority document**, ambiguous
   between two live and unrelated items (`P5-GH-10`, `P6-GH-10`). **Worse than a bare identifier:**
   that sentence opens with **two P5 anchors** (*"SN-13 (P5)"*, *"since P5"*) against **one P6 anchor**
   (*"(P6-M25)"*) — so **neither the identifier nor the context reliably resolves it.** A reader
   weighting salience over adjacency lands on the wrong item.
2. **Escalation notices are cited by milestone key, which cannot identify them.** A milestone can
   raise more than one notice, and **two already share `P10-M34`**. The notice that reported this was
   **itself the second `P11-M36` notice** — it instantiated the ambiguity it reported and counted
   itself in the finding rather than exempting itself. **This milestone has since produced a third
   instance:** `2026-08-05T00_00_00Z__P11-M37__escalation_notice.md` is the first `P11-M37` notice, and
   nothing prevents a second.
3. **The `GH-` prefix inverted mid-corpus.** P5's closure declaration **forward-allocates**
   `P6-GH-10`/`P6-GH-11` (prefix = the phase that *will address* it), while P10 carries `P10-` IDs into
   P11 unchanged (prefix = the phase that *filed* it). **Both readings are live**, and a reader cannot
   tell which one a given ID follows.

## Goals

By the end of this Epic:

1. **No citation in the normative tier resolves to more than one artifact** — `PSG:605` disambiguated,
   no bare `GH-<n>` remaining under `governance/`, escalation notices cited by full filename.
2. **The `GH-` prefix's meaning is fixed and recorded** — it names the phase that **filed** an item,
   permanently, with the forward-allocated ranges ratified as historical exceptions and **not
   renumbered**.
3. **All four rules live in exactly one place** and are cited from elsewhere rather than restated.
4. **The sweep that establishes (1) is exhaustive and evidenced**, distinguishing live IDs from
   pre-renumber historical references.

## Design decisions — decided by the Milestone Chat, apply them

The Milestone Execution Chat Starter assigns *"E37.2's placement of the four rules within the one
section that holds them"* to the Milestone Chat. **Decided here. The Epic applies these and does not
re-litigate them.**

### D1 — A new sibling `##` section, immediately after §Steering Note ID Allocation

**Add one new top-level section to `governance/systems/creation-chat-guide.md`, placed immediately
after §Steering Note ID Allocation ends and before §When to Expect a Progress Digest** — **line 241
post-merge** (was 235 before E37.1 landed; see the re-measurement above). **Locate it by the heading
text `## When to Expect a Progress Digest`, not by the number.**

**Section title: `## Artifact ID Citation Forms`.**

**Reasoning, and why not the two alternatives:**

- **Not *inside* E36.1's §Steering Note ID Allocation.** That section is about **allocating** SN IDs;
  these rules are about **citing** `GH-` IDs and escalation notices. Nesting them there would make the
  section's title false, and would require restructuring merged M36 content — **adjacent to Constraint
  9's boundary, for no benefit.**
- **Not a separate document.** Constraint 6 says *"recorded once, where the corpus states such
  rules"* — and the corpus states such rules **here**, in this document, because E36.1 put the first
  one here. A second document would be a second place to look.
- **A sibling section satisfies "alongside" exactly**: the two rule sets sit adjacent, each citable
  independently, neither nested inside the other, and **E36.1's section is left byte-untouched.**

**The two sections should cross-reference each other** — one line each is enough. They are the same
rule family (*identifiers, and how to cite them unambiguously*) arrived at by two different routes,
and a reader who finds one should be told the other exists.

### D2 — Internal order of the four rules: the general statement first

Within the new section, order the rules **general → specific**, not in the ruling's decision order:

1. **The prefix-meaning statement** (Decision 4) — *the record names the disposition; the identifier
   names the origin.* **This is the premise the others rest on**, and it explains why nothing is
   renumbered.
2. **The ratified historical exceptions** (Decision 4, second half) — `P6-GH-10…15`, `P7-GH-16…21`,
   with the reason they are not renumbered.
3. **The `GH-` phase-prefix citation rule** (Decision 2) — full form in `governance/`, never bare.
4. **The escalation-notice full-filename rule** (Decision 3), generalized to any artifact family keyed
   by level rather than by identifier.

**Reasoning:** a reader arriving at rule 3 without rule 1 will ask *"why does the prefix not change
when the item moves?"* — which is rule 1. The ruling's numbering follows the order the questions were
**asked**; the section should follow the order they are **understood**.
