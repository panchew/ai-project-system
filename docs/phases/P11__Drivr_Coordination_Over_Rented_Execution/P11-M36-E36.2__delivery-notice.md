## Stale claim vs stale pointer — the decision, and its reasoning

**The distinction applied:**

- A **stale pointer** is a citation whose *reference* is out of date while its *statement* stays
  true. It was true at its date. Remedy: **leave the text exactly as issued** and cover it with one
  dated, document-level amendment block naming the old ID, the new ID, the ruling that ordered the
  change, and which occurrences the block covers.
- A **stale factual claim** is a proposition that is **false about the world**, and false
  independently of the rename. **A pointer footnote cannot repair it** — a reader who accepts the
  sentence is misinformed no matter how good the pointer is. Remedy: a dated correction placed
  **adjacent to the claim**, not at the foot of the document, because a reader who reads only that
  bullet must meet the correction. The original sentence stands, unedited.
- A **structured field whose purpose is machine resolution** (front matter) is a third case: leaving
  it stale defeats its only function. Remedy: **update the value and carry the rename inline**, so it
  resolves *and* stays legible as a rename. Applied once — the 2026-07-31 ruling's `concern_id:`,
  now `SN-29 (renumbered from SN-1 on 2026-08-04 by P11-M36-E36.2; see the Amendment note below)`.

**Applied to the case the spec named.** `rulings/2026-08-01__…:124` — *"SN-1 is cited in two
non-normative artifacts (a digest, a ruling)"* — is **both**, and the claim half is the harder half:
the count was wrong when written, and it is wrong by a larger margin now. It carries an adjacent
**Correction** block giving the true inventory (the seven-row table), naming the undercount's shape
(*the ruling did not count itself*), and confirming that **the disposition is unchanged** — seven
non-normative artifacts is still "cheap, contained", and not one of the twenty-one occurrences is in
`governance/`, so the separating rule still sends this collision to *renumber*. The sentence itself
is untouched: the record should show what was believed, with what is true beside it.

**Consistency.** One document-level block per artifact for pointers (seven), one adjacent correction
for the one factual claim, one structured-field update. No prose citation was silently substituted
anywhere.

## The allowlist — mechanism chosen, and how a third collision was demonstrated to fail

**Mechanism: a module-level `dict` mapping ID → the reason it is exempt**, plus a
`unratified_duplicates()` helper that applies it at exactly one place.

**Why a mapping rather than a set, a parametrization, or a fixture.** A set or a marker can hold an
entry with its justification somewhere else — a spec, a commit message, a review thread — and that is
how allowlists rot into blankets. A mapping makes the justification **structurally impossible to
omit**: there is nowhere to put an entry without writing why next to it. The single entry carries HQ
Ruling 2026-08-01 Decision 4, the date-qualification remedy, and the reason renumbering is now
foreclosed — legible to a reader who has never seen the ruling, which is the spec's compatibility
requirement. Extracting `unratified_duplicates()` also let the guard be pointed at a synthetic corpus
without duplicating its logic, which is what makes the demonstration below possible at all.

**`KNOWN_COLLISIONS_REASON` was repurposed, not merely deleted.** It was the `xfail` reason string;
its content became the allowlist's justification text. Nothing remains describing a removed marker.

**How a third collision was demonstrated to fail** — `test_synthetic_third_collision_fails_the_guard`
runs against **the real corpus, not a toy one**: it copies
`.ai-project/artifacts/steering-notes/`, calls the guard to confirm the copy is clean (so any later
failure is caused by the note added, not by the copying), writes one synthetic note claiming `SN-29`,
then asserts **the guard's own assertion fires and names `SN-29`** via `pytest.raises`. It finally
confirms `SN-23` is still tolerated and reported alone — an exception, not an amnesty. The synthetic
duplicate claims `SN-29` deliberately: that is the concrete risk the allocation rule runs, a note
filed between planning and execution quietly taking the ID.

**Mutation evidence — the demonstration is load-bearing, not decorative.** Two deliberate mutations
were introduced, run, and reverted:

| Mutation | Expected to be caught by | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Allowlist made a blanket (`unratified_duplicates` returns `{}`) | `test_synthetic_third_collision_fails_the_guard` | ✅ **fails** — 1 failed, 10 passed |
| Stale entry added (`SN-1` left exempted after its collision cleared) | `test_real_corpus_collisions_are_exactly_the_ratified_set` | ✅ **fails** — 1 failed, 10 passed |
| Neither (restored) | — | ✅ 11 passed |

The second mutation is why that test asserts **equality** rather than containment: it fails from both
sides, so an allowlist entry that outlives its collision cannot survive. Removing a colliding note
now forces removing its entry.
